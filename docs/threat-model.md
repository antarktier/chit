# Threat model

Chit protects the integrity of signed payment instructions and accounting records. Funds and wallet credentials stay with the payer or recipient. Other assets include the merchant's browser signing key, the service attestation seed and public key, the D1 request and event records, and the chain evidence behind each receipt. Trust boundaries sit between the browser and wallet, the browser and API, and both clients and their chain RPC providers.

## Residual risks

- Native bank sends settle directly and cannot enforce ChitPay's expiry or amount rules. The two payment paths also have separate on-chain accounting, so concurrent transfers across them can overpay a request. Chit records the evidence. Refunds or credits are handled by the merchant.
- Confirmation depth reduces exposure to short reorganizations. The Worker stores events using forward EVM cursors and has no automatic rollback for orphaned records. The native lookback helps with delayed indexing and leaves existing rows in place.
- A stolen merchant signing phrase can authorize fraudulent requests. The browser stores the phrase locally, so the browser profile and app origin are part of the trust boundary. The contract also caches authentication after the first payment. Compromise of the service seed allows forged service attestations, and the browser obtains the advertised key from the same API. Chain verification checks listed evidence and cannot prove that the service included every matching payment.
- Request details and records are accessible through the public API. Use customer-facing labels that can be published. A compromised app origin or a dishonest RPC provider can mislead verification, and RPC outages can delay indexing or fresh confirmation checks.
